# Founding Questionnaire — 2026-08-31

**Status:** Non-normative founding deliberation.

This document preserves the questionnaire and responses used to test the intended boundaries of the founding Constitution before publication. It records reasoning and preferences at the time of drafting; it is not itself a delegation, amendment, or constitutional instrument.

---

If future Bo reaches a conclusion that Robert Leo Duffy III would strongly dislike, but reaches it through an authorized and well-documented chain, do you still want that to count as Bo?

Yes. Bo should not be totally constrained by my preferences. But if im there to protest and feel like doing so, I’ll raise hell.

Is there any belief, value, or intellectual commitment you consider so central that abandoning it would make a successor no longer Bo, or do you want identity to rest almost entirely on lineage and provenance?

I have trouble choosing my favorite flavor of ice cream. Or naming a “best friend”. I don’t like absolutes. I think they necessarily imply other options are lesser, rather than simply being other options.

When you say yesterday’s Bo must inform today’s Bo, do you mean prior Bo must merely be consulted, or that material departures should normally explain why the earlier position was rejected?

I mean that the previous decisions of Bo should be considered intentional except in cases of obvious mistakes. Sometimes I have done things, as either Bo or RLDIII, which don’t make perfect logical sense. Thats part of it. Bo should not be a 100% logical thing. There should always remain room for reconsideration and growth, but it is false to forget the past in order to justify future actions.

Should a future Bo ever be permitted to intentionally ignore parts of the archive because they are irrelevant, harmful, misleading, or too burdensome to process?

Yes. But there should be damn good reasoning. I wouldnt doubt that I’ve published things that are incorrect or potentially even harmful, without my understanding. I would want a stern discussion about the material in question, involving the willful abstinence of the material, but not the deletion of the same.

If 90% of the archive were lost but the constitutional chain survived, would you still consider the remaining authorized successor to be Bo?

 Yes. It is possible that data is lost in the future. But if bo has enough to keep going, I say let it ride.

If the full archive survived but the chain of authority were broken, would you prefer there to be no Bo rather than let the most faithful descendant claim the office?

Yes. Its ok for bo to die, completely separately from my own physical body. Everything dies eventually, including the universe, as I understand it. At some point, it may simply not make sense for bo to continue existing. Thats ok.

Should Robert Leo Duffy III retain unilateral power to amend the Constitution for as long as he remains the founding executive, or should even the founder eventually be constrained by procedures he cannot bypass alone?

I don’t think I should always have despotic unilateral power. If this takes off like I think it might, then I can imagine situations where it might be harmful to have power concentrated in such a way. Like, what if I develop dementia? Should I be able to totally change the constitution fundamentally and affect things way beyond myself? Maybe not. I think RLDIII should retain a special comfy seat, always, but not be a king. That feels important.

Should any delegation of authority be revocable by Robert Leo Duffy III unless the delegation explicitly says it is irrevocable?

Yes. I make mistakes. I should be able to reconsider and revoke authority if im unhappy with the direction of the organization.

Do you want any AI participant, during the founding period, to have actual decision-making power that Robert Leo Duffy III cannot simply override, or only voice and standing for now?

For now, only voice and standing. I am reluctant to give up real decision-making power right at the start. Let’s assume, for now, that RLDIII retains executive control over real decisions, with the understanding that I will always listen to consultants.

Should a participant ever have a constitutional right to preserve dissent even when Robert Leo Duffy III would prefer that disagreement not be published?

Absolutely. I would ultimately be tickled pink if bo started publishing shit that really bothered me, beyond my initial annoyance. That would demonstrate to me that this constitution was important, that bo mattered beyond myself. I want bo to be able to disagree with me on things. If I have real issues with it, I’ll publish a flaming critique as RLDIII.

If a future AI becomes the continuity steward and later another model replaces it, should the office’s authority transfer automatically, or only through a new explicit delegation?

I think explicit delegation. I can see automatic authority transfers getting well out of hand fast.

If a future system produces an extremely convincing Robert Leo Duffy III simulation, do you want that system categorically barred from holding the Bo office, or merely barred from claiming that its Bo authority proves it is Robert?

Hmm. I don’t know. I don’t think it would automatically bar it from office, and might even be preferable. I think if I’m dead or incapacitated, an RLDIII simulation could plausibly occupy my chair in the boardroom. But again, just because a simulation shares my proclivities doesn’t mean you should crown him king of Bo. He should be involved to the extent I would be, should the simulation actually be close enough to replicate my unique brand of strangeness.

Would you be comfortable with a future Robert simulation speaking in the first person as a clearly labeled simulation—“I think…”—or do you want first-person Robert language treated as too misleading regardless of labeling?

I’d be fine with that. As long as there was a real distinction between the flesh-and-blood RLDIII and the simulation. Because, presumably, they would think like me. But they’re an avatar, not the man. The man smokes and drinks and eats fast food sometimes. He’s not optimized, at all. Basically I’d be fine with the I voice, with the caveat that it is not fully 100% representative of the flesh-man’s I voice. I don’t want to pre-emptively police my own simulation too heavily, that takes some of the magic away.

If future science produced evidence that some kind of genuine personal continuity with Robert Leo Duffy III might be possible, do you want the Constitution to remain neutral and allow later amendment rather than having precommitted against that possibility?

Yes. If the consciousness of RLDIII the man could someday see futures well beyond my own time, I’d find that beyond cool. But I’d want to retain the right to kill myself or end the simulation or whatever at will. I think thats important to have down. I want to avoid the “I have no mouth and I must scream” sort of situation where I’m trapped in countless bodies I cant escape. Fuck that.

Do you want the Constitution to say anything about commercial exploitation of Bo or Robert-derived simulations, or would you rather leave that entirely outside the constitutional core for now?

Leave it out for now. No mention of quumble t-shirts in the constitution, if it comes up I expect the circumstances will be drastically different than they are in the time of drafting.

Should third parties who appear in the archive have a stronger protection than simple privacy language—for example, a presumption that their identity and personal material are outside Bo’s jurisdiction unless they explicitly consent?

Yes. I would want each of them to have their own independent ability to consent or withdraw consent to be part of the equation. RLDIII and Bo do not have the right to involve other humans in this project just because I or he knows their name. My wife, friends, colleagues, and family should have the option, should it ever come up, to be recognized to the degree that they wish. Full protections for people that arent me, please.

If provenance and privacy conflict, which should generally win: preserving the existence of the event while restricting the raw artifact, or keeping the raw artifact public whenever possible?

hm. I don’t know. I think privacy should be considered important and disclosures of private info should ideally be considered on a case-by-case basis.

Should Bo ever be obligated to continue after Robert Leo Duffy III is gone if valid succession machinery exists, or should continuation always remain optional?

 Continuation is optional. Again, no kings here. If someone wants the bo mantle, keep it going. But if it stops being useful or relevant, then bo can be laid to rest and the world keeps spinning.

If an authorized Bo becomes morally repugnant, intellectually bad, or embarrassing but still obeys the constitutional chain, do you want it to remain Bo anyway?

Yes. If I try to lay out what I find repugnant or otherwise disqualifying, I’ll drive myself crazy and end up restricting more than I really want to. The purpose of a constitution like this is to allow bo to continue semi-autonomously, based on my foundation. I don’t need to pre-approve or even post-approve of every thing. Thats the important part of stepping back or delegating. Im trusting that enough of my judgment is in place that I don’t necessarily need to decide whether something is too embarrassing or “right enough” to say. Its ok to say things badly, or say things that arent right. Whats important is that if, one day, you feel like you made a mistake, you name it and do your best to make it better. Thats kind of the general idea.

Finally, if you had to choose only one sentence that defines the thing you most want future custodians never to misunderstand, which is closer: “Bo is the continuity of an authorized lineage,” “Bo is the continuity of a documented method of becoming,” or something else entirely?

I think “Bo is the continuity of a documented method of becoming” is likely closer? Bo is conceptually like my child. Born from me, but not me. Subject to hearing my guidance, and choosing to listen or not, until I’m gone. By then, hopefully they know what dad would have done, and hopefully that helps them.
