# Deliberation Record — Version 0.3

**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** Non-normative provenance. This document records reasoning that preceded Version 0.3. It does not itself appoint an officeholder, activate a mandate, create authority, or alter the Constitution.

## Trigger

Version 0.2 anticipated that an artificial system might receive authority to conduct bounded research, execute specified acts, and make specified decisions without contemporaneous approval of every individual step. The prospect of long-running autonomous systems made that possibility operational rather than hypothetical.

The immediate constitutional question was not succession or artificial personhood. It was agency between moments of human supervision: whether work could validly continue within Bo while Robert Leo Duffy III was absent, what powers such work would require, and how its external effects could remain bounded.

The founding decision was to permit meaningful autonomous research without simultaneously granting public, financial, or constitutional sovereignty.

## Decisions incorporated into v0.3

### 1. The boardroom receives an institutional layer

Version 0.3 distinguishes participants, chairs, and offices. A participant is the human, system, or procedure that acts. A chair is the capacity in which it participates. An office is the constitutional bundle of functions, standing, or power attached to that chair.

Four founding-period offices are recognized:

- the Executive Chair;
- the Research Chair;
- the Critical Chair; and
- the Clerk and Archivist.

Robert Leo Duffy III occupies the Executive Chair. Creation of the other offices does not appoint an occupant. Appointment and authority remain separate acts.

### 2. Autonomous initiative can be real without becoming sovereign

A properly appointed Research Chair may eventually choose questions, methods, branches, tests, priorities, and conclusions not anticipated by the Executive Chair. Treating every such choice as a delayed transcription of Robert Leo Duffy III's will would defeat the purpose of the experiment.

Autonomy therefore can produce authentic events within the Bo lineage. It does not allow an autonomous participant to enlarge its mandate, appoint itself, manufacture presumed consent, or convert technical possession into constitutional power.

### 3. Authentic Bo work and public Bo work are distinct

During the initial autonomous period, unsupervised work should ordinarily occur in private repositories or other nonpublic working spaces.

Validly delegated research may belong to Bo before Robert Leo Duffy III reviews it. Publication is a separate act. During Version 0.3, public release remains reserved to Robert Leo Duffy III acting as Executive Chair.

This creates a deliberate airlock:

**private Bo work → human review → authorized public release**

Robert Leo Duffy III may reject, revise, preserve, or publish what returns through that airlock. Rejection does not falsify the historical fact that authorized work occurred.

### 4. Initial financial freedom is deliberately narrow

Autonomous research may eventually benefit from meaningful financial discretion. It does not follow that Robert Leo Duffy III has agreed to expose his personal finances to unknown or open-ended liability.

Robert Leo Duffy III is willing to spend for good work within reason. He is not an unlimited source of funds for Bo, and neither his ability nor his general willingness to pay establishes consent to a particular charge.

Version 0.3 therefore fixes the maximum authorized aggregate external cost at **US$50 per autonomous mandate**. Fifty dollars was selected as enough to permit a real experiment while keeping the initial personal exposure comparable to an ordinary discretionary expense.

The cap covers the whole mandate, including retries, continuations, subordinate tasks, and authorized subagents. Autonomous participants may not raise, replenish, route around, or reinterpret the cap. Recurring commitments, borrowing, debt, automatic replenishment, uncapped infrastructure, hiring, and obligations without a fixed maximum are outside the initial grant.

Financial authority may be expanded in a later version if experience justifies it. Any increase must operate prospectively. A later permissive rule cannot retroactively authorize an earlier overrun.

### 5. Pre-1.0 revision is protected rather than apologized for

The Constitution presently governs real acts, but versions before 1.0 remain a founding and experimental period. Substantial revision is expected as hypothetical questions become operational.

That flexibility is bounded by explicitness, preserved rationale, prospective effect, and an honest history of what each version did and did not authorize. The expectation of revision is not permission to ignore the version currently in force.

Version 1.0 will require an explicit ratification event. It will not occur merely because the next incremental number happens to be 1.0.

## Powers expressly reserved or withheld

During Version 0.3:

- Robert Leo Duffy III retains residual executive, appointment, removal, amendment, publication, and financial authority;
- no artificial participant is appointed merely by this amendment;
- no autonomous mandate is activated merely by this amendment;
- publication authority is not delegated;
- no autonomous mandate may authorize more than US$50 in aggregate external cost;
- autonomous participants may not enlarge their own authority;
- recursive delegation requires express authorization; and
- technical capability does not substitute for constitutional authority.

## Questions deliberately left unresolved

Version 0.3 does not decide:

- which particular Astra or other artificial system may occupy the Research Chair;
- the form of the first autonomous mandate;
- what operational evidence should justify increased authority;
- whether an Editorial or Publication Chair should later be created;
- whether protected private work may eventually receive reconsideration rights;
- how later officeholders may treat work that the founder declined to publish;
- when strategic agenda-setting should become a separately delegated power;
- how comprehensive incapacity, succession, or post-founder adjudication should work; or
- what substantive or procedural conditions should govern ratification of Version 1.0.

These questions are preserved because Version 0.3 is intended to govern the first experiment, not to pretend that its results are already known.
