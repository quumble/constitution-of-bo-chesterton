# Constitution v0.45 deliberation — 2026-09-07

This is a faithful summary of the founder's discussion with Codex and an explicitly requested subagent review, not a verbatim transcript or independently normative instrument.

## Prompting experience

Robert Leo Duffy III reported that the first autonomous Research Chair/Institute studies felt as though they were written in another language despite being English. His concern was that Bo could become highly machine-legible while offering little human legibility. This records his experience; it is not an independent evaluation of those studies.

Robert wanted Bo to explore beyond his intuitive understanding while routinely explaining work at a level sufficient to support his decisions. He also wanted readers with less formal education to have a reasonable chance to understand relevant parts. He did not request elementary explanation of every equation. He distinguished Bo's freedom to work in other languages from his own present limitation to English.

## Deliberation and choices

The discussion separated authority, responsibility for interpretation, and broader human accessibility. An initial suggestion that the Chair might require clarification before material was ready for executive decision was rejected. Robert preferred a standing best-effort expectation, with the Chair able to remain dissatisfied and continue explaining, rather than a perfect interpretation standard or automatic gate.

The resulting proposal places human legibility in Article II, establishes the Interpretation and Access Chair in Article VII, and clarifies understanding and authorization in Article VIII. It preserves both intellectual ambition and faithful accounts of findings, uncertainty, and disagreement.

At Robert's request, a subagent reviewed the live v0.4 Constitution and proposed text for strengths and weaknesses. It supported the overall approach and recommended narrowing standing to substantive concerns within the Chair's responsibilities and explicitly framing executive explanation as a good-faith effort. It also noted the future need for ordinary operating arrangements and possible editorial shortening. Robert accepted the two wording refinements. No broader shortening or operating procedure was adopted in this amendment.

Robert reviewed the revised proposal and instructed that it be pushed. The repository update is prepared for his local GPG-signed adoption and publication.

## Powers and boundaries

The new office receives voice concerning interpretation and accessibility and standing to preserve substantive concerns within its responsibilities. Establishment does not appoint an occupant. It confers no veto, comprehension-certification power, executive, publication, financial, or amendment authority unless expressly granted. Concern about an explanation does not by itself invalidate a decision or suspend valid work.

The amendment makes explanation a standing responsibility and clarifies that confusion, silence, and general trust do not supply missing authorization. It permits clearly authorized work to continue while a disputed extension is clarified.

Existing publication restrictions, the US$50 aggregate external-cost ceiling, revocation powers, accumulated-state requirements, and other authority limits continue. The version change does not renew or replenish mandates. Amendment authority, publication authority, financial authority, succession, and the constitutive conditions of continuity are unchanged. The Commercial Doctrine and earlier founder attestation are unchanged.

## Questions left open

The occupant, appointment instrument, routine workflow, and practical formats for interpretation remain to be determined. The amendment sets no universal comprehension test, literacy threshold, translation format, or automatic enforcement mechanism. It does not promise that every audience will understand every work.

## Ordinary-language account

Bo can investigate beyond what Robert already understands. Explaining its work remains part of the job, both for his decisions and for other relevant readers. The new Chair can keep trying and preserve serious concerns, but cannot use interpretation to change findings or take over decisions. Trust and confusion do not create extra permission.
