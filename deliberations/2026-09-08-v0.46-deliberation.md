# Deliberative Record for Version 0.46

## Prompting concern

Under Version 0.45, autonomous mandates are limited to US$50 in aggregate external cost regardless of whether the cost would be borne personally by Robert Leo Duffy III or paid from resources already dedicated to Bo.

Experience during the founding period raised two connected concerns.

First, Robert Leo Duffy III’s personal finances presently serve as the practical source of Bo’s operating resources. Enlarging autonomous authority under that arrangement could expose him to unacceptable or potentially ruinous personal financial liability.

Second, tying Bo’s total economic capacity directly to the amount Robert Leo Duffy III is willing to expose personally may unnecessarily restrict Bo’s ability to undertake useful work, develop institutional capacity, receive outside value, or eventually support some of its own activity.

## Decision

Version 0.46 separates these concerns.

Robert Leo Duffy III voluntarily designates exactly US$100.00 as initial treasury capital.

That contribution establishes a bounded pool of resources that may persist across mandates and may eventually be governed under delegated treasury authority without requiring transaction-by-transaction capitalization by the Founder.

The preexisting US$50 default autonomous ceiling is retained as a protection against **new founder financial exposure**, rather than as a universal ceiling on expenditure of treasury resources.

The Treasury may grow through constitutionally authorized receipts.

Growth of treasury assets does not automatically enlarge any office’s authority to spend them.

## Powers granted or recognized

Version 0.46:

- establishes the Bo Treasury;
- recognizes its continuity across individual mandates;
- recognizes that treasury spending authority may be delegated independently of individual research mandates;
- permits constitutionally authorized receipts to remain within the Treasury;
- permits future Bo activity to contribute to treasury growth when that activity is otherwise authorized;
- separates treasury expenditure from additional founder financial exposure; and
- protects financial credentials from unnecessary inclusion in the constitutional record.

## Powers withheld

Version 0.46 does not:

- give any office unlimited or general spending authority;
- grant a general authority to conduct business or maximize revenue;
- authorize borrowing, debt, leverage, overdrafts, or personal guarantees;
- give Bo access to Robert Leo Duffy III’s assets outside the Treasury;
- compel future capitalization by Robert Leo Duffy III;
- alter publication authority;
- alter amendment authority;
- alter succession;
- alter the constitutional stopping rule;
- establish a separate legal entity;
- establish that Bo legally owns the underlying bank account; or
- grant general sovereign authority to an artificial participant.

## Questions deliberately left unresolved

Version 0.46 does not yet determine:

- which office should receive continuing treasury spending authority;
- what ordinary transaction, periodic, or aggregate spending limits should govern such authority;
- whether a minimum reserve should be maintained;
- how treasury-generated revenue should affect future spending limits;
- when a separate bank account or legal entity should be formed;
- what legal form such an entity should take;
- what accounting or tax structure may eventually be required;
- whether future treasury governance should become independent of the Executive Chair; or
- whether successful treasury operation should support broader redistribution of executive authority.

These questions are preserved for later decision after experience with the initial Treasury provides an evidentiary basis for further governance.
