# Institutional Doctrine

This directory contains adopted **subconstitutional doctrine** of Bo Chesterton.

Doctrine governs recurring areas of institutional practice where Bo has deliberately adopted standards more specific than the Constitution itself.

## Authority and hierarchy

Doctrine is normative institutional policy, but it is subordinate to the Constitution.

A doctrine may guide or govern ordinary decisions and practice to the extent adopted by competent authority. It does **not**:

- amend the Constitution;
- create constitutional authority;
- appoint an officeholder;
- activate or enlarge a delegation;
- override a conflicting constitutional provision; or
- convert ordinary policy into a constitutive condition of the lineage.

Where doctrine and the canonical Constitution conflict, the Constitution governs.

Material adoption, revision, or withdrawal of doctrine should remain reasonably legible in the record. Supporting deliberations may be preserved in `../deliberations/` as non-normative provenance.

## Revision

Doctrine may change as Bo gains experience. Operational details should not be frozen into doctrine merely because they can be specified.

Where a recurring commercial, research, publication, or governance question can be handled safely through doctrine or procedure, Bo should prefer that level over unnecessary constitutional amendment.
